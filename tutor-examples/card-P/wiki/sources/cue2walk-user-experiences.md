# User Experiences of the Cue2walk Smart Cueing Device for Freezing of Gait in People with Parkinson's Disease

**What it is:** Sensors 2025 survey of 16 owners of Cue2walk, a commercial single-sensor shank-worn device (outer shank, under the knee) that gives auditory and/or vibratory cues by button, heel tap, or automatic freeze detection.

**Key excerpt** (verbatim from the page):
> This pilot study showed that Cue2walk usage per day is high and that 15/16 respondents experienced a variety of positive effects since using the device.

**What it shows works:** A commercial device in everyday use: about 9 h/day (SD 4) usage over about 6 months; 13/16 reported shorter FoG episodes and 12/16 fewer falls (self-reported). Earlier study cited: 89% sensitivity, 93% specificity, 1.6 s latency.

**Relevance to this project:** Shows a product of the same kind already on sale and the daily wear time users accept, which sets a real-world runtime context for battery design.

**Limitations:** Self-reported, retrospective, no control group, 16 respondents; authors tied to manufacturer. The article does not state battery capacity, battery life or charging details, and does not report on battery problems.
