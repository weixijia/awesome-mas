# Good vibrations: tactile cueing for freezing of gait in Parkinson's disease

**What it is:** Klaver et al., J Neurol 2023;270(7):3424-3432. Compared tactile cueing (vibrating socks, open and closed loop) with auditory cueing and no cueing in 31 people with PD who have freezing of gait, tested ON and OFF medication. The PMC page was blocked by a browser check, so the content below comes from the Europe PMC record of the same paper (abstract only, full text not read).

**Key excerpt** (verbatim from the page):
> Cueing did not improve FOG at the group level; however, tactile as well as auditory cueing improved FOG in many individuals.

**What it shows works:** Mean time frozen without cueing was 11.2% (ON) and 21.5% (OFF). No cue type changed percentage of time frozen (ON p = 0.20, OFF p = 0.12), nor FOG episode counts or gait parameters. 22 of 31 participants responded (relative reduction of more than 10% in time frozen), but which cue worked varied widely between individuals.

**Relevance to this project:** Informs what to expect from a vibration cue's effect on freezing, including large individual variation in response.

**Limitations:** Cue delivered by vibrating socks, not an ankle band. Group-level null result. Abstract-level reading only; motor specifications not extracted.
