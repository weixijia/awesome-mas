# P3 — neuro physiotherapist, NHS community team (FICTIONAL)

Setting: interview at clinic, 25 min, 2026-10-20.

- "Cueing works for many patients, but not all. Some respond to sound, some to rhythm on the skin, some to visual lines."
- "Rhythm matters — we usually set it near their normal walking cadence, then adjust."
- "Patients habituate. If the cue is always on, it stops working. On-demand is better in theory."
- On detection: "Freezing often starts with shuffling and shortened steps — you can see it coming a second or two before."
- Safety: "A cue at the wrong moment, like on stairs, could make someone hurry and fall."
- "Many of my patients have tremor and stiff fingers — small buttons and tight straps are a real problem."
