# Identification of anatomical locations: its relevance for vibrotactile perception of individuals with Parkinson's disease

**What it is:** Raghuvanshi et al., BioMedical Engineering OnLine 24, article 21 (2025). Authors' own vibrotactile system tested on 21 people with PD and 21 age-matched healthy people, comparing how well vibration location was perceived across body sites. The Springer page redirected, so content was read via the Europe PMC record (abstract level).

**Key excerpt** (verbatim from the page; fragment as returned by the fetch tool, which limits quoting length):
> In contrast, for individuals with PD, the 'Thigh' emerged as a strong candidate anatomical location

**What it shows works:** Healthy group identified wrist and thigh 100% and finger 98% correctly. In the PD group the thigh was identified correctly 96% and the wrist 92%; only the thigh was significantly better (p < 0.05) than all other locations except the wrist. The abstract notes past work compared sites including shin, calf, ankle, Achilles tendon and heel, but only in healthy people.

**Relevance to this project:** Informs whether vibration at the ankle/shank region is perceived correctly by people with PD, relative to other body sites.

**Limitations:** Abstract-level reading. Ankle-specific accuracy numbers for the PD group were not captured. Custom stimulation hardware; motor type and frequency not extracted.
