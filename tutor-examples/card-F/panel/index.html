File: outputs/2026-11-05-feasibility-stepcue.md · Feasibility review: StepCue · 2026-11-05

Idea reviewed: A small wearable that notices when a person with Parkinson's is about to "freeze" while walking and gives a rhythmic vibration cue on the wrist or ankle to help them start walking again.

Overall verdict: Research-grade · Overall difficulty 5 (driven by: pre-freeze prediction, before or at onset) · Confidence medium

Component ratings (from feasibility-reviewer)

ComponentCritical?MaturityDiff.Why, for this team (short)
1. Motion sensing (ankle/shank IMU)YesProven2A cheap IMU (MPU-6050) is already used in a small wearable. Member B's basic Arduino skills make I2C wiring learnable. Daphnet is accelerometer-only and lab-recorded. Confidence medium.
2. Real-time FoG detection on a microcontrollerYesAdaptable4A CNN+RNN ran at 40 Hz on a Cortex-M7 with 261 ms average delay, but the system was personalized (210–351 s of training data per patient) and the team has signal-processing, ML and low-power firmware gaps. Confidence medium.
3. Pre-freeze prediction (before or at onset)YesResearch-grade5The one study found: Pre-FOG windows alone reached 55.2% sensitivity, and its best result needed insole pressure sensors plus shank and thigh IMUs; only 7 participants froze. No reliable route to prediction found within the timeline. Confidence medium.
4. Vibrotactile rhythmic cue (motor, driver, placement)YesProven2DRV2605L and the Adafruit library are catalogue parts that fit the team's Arduino level. Clinical evidence is mixed (33-patient pilot positive; 31-person sock study no group-level effect). No ankle-specific perception data. Confidence medium for hardware.
5. Power, battery and wearable form factorYesAdaptable3ParCuR: 79.3 mA (motor off) / 151.7 mA (on) on 300 mAh, est. 225 / 120 min, Wi-Fi not BLE. Cue2walk users wear their device ~9 h/day. Low-power firmware gap and £300 budget are the limits. Confidence medium-low.
6. Data, ethics and testing with usersYesAdaptable4Public labelled data exists (Daphnet, CC BY 4.0). Testing with people with Parkinson's needs ethics approval; no clinical collaborator beyond an informal PhD contact. Low confidence on the approval timeline: No evidence found.

Technical routes for pre-freeze prediction (unranked)

  • Wizard-of-Oz trigger — a person or simulated signal fires the cue; the detection claim is not tested.
  • Detection at or just after onset — what the demonstrated systems do (261 ms delay); does not predict.
  • Add plantar pressure sensing — best Pre-FOG result; changes the form factor, adds parts and power.
  • Foot-contact-triggered cueing — as in the foot pilot; changes what the device does.
  • Narrowed scope — single user, personalized model, controlled doorway or simulated FoG.

The team decides which route, if any, to take.

Questions the team needs to answer (from sparring-critic)

  1. What does "imminent" mean in measurable terms, for example the lead time before onset your detector must reach? On which data will you measure it, and what is the sensitivity/specificity at that lead time?
  2. With ML/data skills of 0 and 1 and no personalised training data, who will train, quantise and deploy the model on the MCU? Have you run any model, or even a threshold rule, on your board at the needed rate?
  3. What is the end-to-end latency from the start of the freeze to felt vibration on skin, measured with your actual motor, driver, strap and window length? What do you do with that number if it exceeds one second?
  4. What false-cue rate per hour of ordinary walking can a user tolerate, and how will you measure it without access to participants with Parkinson's before ethics approval arrives?
  5. What will the exhibition prototype actually demonstrate to visitors without FoG, and which claims will it make about detection versus cueing?

Evidence gaps

  • Component 3: one B-grade paper, no code, 7 participants who froze.
  • Component 6: No evidence found on ethics or approval timelines.
  • Component 4: no ankle-specific vibration perception data in PD; no motor current figures.
  • Component 5: one lab estimate (Wi-Fi, external processing); no BLE or on-device ML power data.
  • Component 2: no open-source FoG firmware for small MCUs found.
  • raw/ holds only its README, so no team material is cited.

Full text, including the critic's assumptions, failure modes and disagreements, is in the file linked above.
